Abstract. Assuming G¨odel’s axiom of constructibility V = L, we present a characterization of those groups L for which there exist arbitrarily large groups H such
that Aut(H) ∼= L. In particular, we show that it suffices to have one such group H
such that the size of its center is bigger than 2|L|+ℵ0 .
§ 0. Introduction
The representation problem from group theory asks:
Problem 0.1. For a given group L, is there a group H such that Aut(H) ∼= L?
There are a lot of interesting research papers in this area. Here, we recall only a short
list of them. The study of automorphism groups of finite abelian groups started with
Shoda [13]. Hallett–Hirsch [8] and Corner [3] classified finite groups which are the automorphism group of some torsion-free group. For infinite groups, Beaumont–Pierce [2]
studied automorphism groups of torsion-free groups of rank two. May [10] has more
results on abelian automorphism groups of torsion-free groups of countable rank.
A related problem is the classical problem of establishing whether two algebraic
structures are isomorphic when their automorphism groups are isomorphic. Thanks
to [9], the answer is yes for abelian p-groups, when p 6 = 2. Also, by the work of
Corner–Goldsmith [4], the answer for reduced torsion-free modules over the ring Jp
of p-adic integers, where p 6 = 2, is affirmative. The question of whether two abelian
2-groups are isomorphic when their automorphism groups are isomorphic is still an
open problem. For an excellent survey on these and related topics, see Fuchs [6].

Despite lots of work, the study of the automorphism group of groups is not documented very well, and there is no universal solution to Problem 0.1. There are several
examples of groups that can never be the automorphism group of some abelian group,
so that Problem 0.1 reduces to characterizing those groups L that can be the automorphism group of another group.
Throughout this paper, we work with arbitrary groups, so they are not required to
be abelian. In order to study Problem 0.1, we study the structure of the automorphism
group and inner automorphisms of a given group. It may be worth noting that Baer [1]
studied the normal subgroup structure of Aut(H) for infinite p-groups H. For more
details on this and further achievements, see again the book of Fuchs [6].
We suggest a good version of the problem:
Problem 0.2. Characterize the groups L such that for arbitrarily large cardinals λ
there is a group H of cardinal λ such that Aut(H) ∼= L.
We approach Problem 0.2 as follows. Given groups L and H and a monomorphism
F : L → Aut(H), our aim is to find a group H′ ⊇ H and an isomorphism F ′ :
L → Aut(H′) in such a way that for each ℓ ∈ L, F ′(ℓ) is an automorphism of H′ which
extends F (ℓ). Thus, we have to find H′ in such a way that, for each ℓ ∈ L, F (ℓ) extends
to an automorphism of H′, and such that if h ∈ Aut(H) is not in the range of F, then
h can not be extended to an automorphism of H′; furthermore, any automorphism of
H′ must result from extending an automorphism of H. We show that, under some
extra assumptions on our initial setup, this is always possible.
